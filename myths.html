Myths and facts

Stigma stops people from asking for help. Here are common myths and what we know.

Myth: Mental health problems are a sign of weakness.

Fact: They are health conditions and can affect anyone. Asking for help is a strong, practical step.

Myth: Young people just need to “be strong” or “pray more.”

Fact: Faith and family can be real sources of strength, and they work best alongside professional care when it is needed.

Myth: Talking about suicide puts the idea in someone’s head.

Fact: Asking caringly and listening does not cause harm. It can open the door to help.

Myth: Counseling is only for people in a crisis.

Fact: Counseling also helps with stress, grief, bullying, and big life changes.

Myth: Help costs too much.

Fact: Many crisis lines are free, and some clinics offer free or reduced-cost options. See free listings.

Myth: Seeking help means my school or family will find out and judge me.

Fact: You can ask a counselor how privacy works before you share. Read about your rights.

Sources: see References (World Health Organization; National Center for Mental Health).
