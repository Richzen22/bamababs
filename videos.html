Video library

Five short, silent videos with on-screen text and captions. Each has a text version underneath.

Box breathing: slow down in one minute

A one-minute breathing guide with a growing and shrinking circle.

Text version (transcript)
  1. Sit comfortably. Feet on the floor. Shoulders loose.
  2. Breathe in through your nose for a count of 4.
  3. Hold gently for 4.
  4. Breathe out through your mouth for 4.
  5. Rest for 4. Repeat three times.
  6. Notice how you feel. If you feel dizzy, breathe normally.

Silent video with on-screen text and captions. Original work, see attribution.

Grounding: the 5-4-3-2-1 skill

Use your senses to come back to the room when thoughts race.

Text version (transcript)
  1. Name 5 things you see.
  2. Name 4 things you feel or touch.
  3. Name 3 things you hear.
  4. Name 2 things you smell.
  5. Name 1 thing you taste, or take one slow breath.

Silent video with on-screen text and captions. Original work, see attribution.

Release tension: a gentle body reset

Four simple muscle releases. Skip any move that hurts.

Text version (transcript)
  1. Lift shoulders toward ears for 5, then drop.
  2. Make fists for 5, then open.
  3. Scrunch your face for 5, then soften.
  4. Press feet into the floor for 5, then release.
  5. Take one slow breath out.

Silent video with on-screen text and captions. Original work, see attribution.

What to expect when you call a crisis line

Takes the fear out of making the first call.

Text version (transcript)
  1. You do not need the right words.
  2. Dial 1553 (toll-free) or another number on the Crisis help page.
  3. A trained person answers and listens.
  4. Share as much or as little as you like.
  5. They ask how you are and help you plan a next step.
  6. In danger right now? Call 911.

Silent video with on-screen text and captions. Original work, see attribution.

Being there for a friend

Five small steps for supporting a classmate who seems down.

Text version (transcript)
  1. Ask: how are you, really?
  2. Listen without rushing to fix.
  3. Say: I am glad you told me.
  4. Offer to go with them to the guidance office or call a hotline.
  5. Keep checking in. If they are in danger, call 911 and tell an adult.
